## Cross-cutting observations

**Density choreography.** Every stage is a sawtooth: popcorn build (~30–60s) → elite/set-piece spike → full-screen cancel (midboss kill, BREAK bomb) → 5–15s item-shower valley → rebuild. Valleys are *rewarding*, not empty: green ✕ gems and gold bars rain during them (4:36–4:58, 15:00–15:20). Pre-boss valleys are consistent: ~6–10s of empty background + dialogue card (2:02, 5:16, 10:46, 16:00, 23:00).

**Tension-release mechanics.** Release is always a *conversion*: bullets → gold squares/pills on midboss kill (1:20, 9:20, 20:24), BREAK bomb → +1,000,000 cancel (4:16, 16:28), phase kill → PERFECT bonus + shower (5:24, 23:40). The game never just fades bullets out; it pays you for surviving them.

**Boss grammar.** Named attack captions under the HP bar; 3→6 phases scaling by stage; phase transitions telegraphed 3 ways at once (caption swap, sprite chunk destruction or form change, bullet-color change); later bosses add arena-level telegraphs (red room 17:36, sky cycle 23:14–25:56). Bosses hold top-center; repositioning happens *between* phases behind smoke/wake (5:24, 11:12).

**Color language.** Enemy bullets live in 4–5 high-contrast families that never change roles: red/white rings, pink/magenta orbs, gold pills/bars, green shards, black dots with white outline. Items are unmistakably NOT bullets: green ✕ gems, gold squares, medals. Popcorn enemies are white/light against dark fields and dark against light fields — the *background* is tuned to keep sprites readable (darkest backdrop, S4, has the brightest enemy palette).

**Place identity.** 5 stages = 5 palettes (green mountain, blue harbor, teal→brown→night sky, black/brown factory, moss ruins→red abstraction). And *within* every stage the backdrop changes 2–4 times (S1 river→walls→canyon; S3 day→storm→fortress→night→red; S5 ruins→night forest→polygon cave→sunset arena). Set pieces are anchored to background landmarks (battleship 5:00, fortress deck 8:14) and the abstract-void treatment is reserved for boss arenas and final approaches.

## Principles for an homage (SPEEDHELL: one-stage 320×427 Psikyo-style speed-kill danmaku, 8 sections: popcorn intro → turret alley → mid gauntlet → midboss → rush → elite pair → release → 3-phase boss)

1. **Re-skin the background at every section seam — palette first, geometry second.** BRDA never lets 60 seconds pass without the backdrop changing identity: S1 goes river→walled gardens→dark canyon inside 50s (0:16→0:48); S3 runs day clouds→rust storm→fortress deck→night storm (6:48, 7:42, 8:14, 9:24); S5 drains from moss ruins to abstract red polygons for the final approach (21:16). SPEEDHELL's single unchanging starfield is exactly the thing BRDA proves you can't skip — even a starfield can hue-shift, gain a planet limb, debris field, or station hull per section. **Missing — this is the #1 gap.** Eight sections should read as eight *places*, with the cheapest possible move being a per-section tint + one parallax landmark.

2. **Anchor at least one section to a landmark that scrolls through it.** The S2 battleship destroyed bow-to-stern (5:00–5:10) and the S3 fortress deck that hosts the whole turret alley plus the midboss (8:14–9:20) fuse background and gameplay: the *place* is the wave. SPEEDHELL's turret alley and midboss are the natural hosts — mount the turrets on a hull that slides under the starfield, and let the midboss be a piece of it. **Missing.**

3. **Escalate a motif by repetition-with-density, not novelty.** S1 plays its garden-wall turret motif 3–4 times (0:26–0:46), then replays it denser after the midboss (1:22–1:46); S5's miniboss *chains* (18:46→19:24, 20:02→20:24) are the midboss idea run twice back-to-back. A section should state its gimmick, repeat it 2–3 times escalating, then hand off. SPEEDHELL's 8-section grammar already does this at macro scale — **partially followed**; check each section internally repeats its wave shape ≥2× before the peak.

4. **Make every release a conversion, never a fade.** Every BRDA valley is paid for: midboss kill converts the live bullet field to gold (1:20, 9:20, 20:24), BREAK bombs print +1,000,000 cancels (4:16), phase kills stamp PERFECT bonuses (23:40), and valleys rain items (4:36–4:58). SPEEDHELL's "release" section should be an item/score shower triggered by the elite pair's death cancel — survival must feel lucrative, not merely quiet. **Follows if the release section cancels-to-items; missing if it's just a lull.**

5. **Give the boss one signature bullet shape per phase, and telegraph phase changes three ways at once.** BRDA phases pair a caption swap with visible sprite destruction/form change AND a bullet-vocabulary swap (DELPHI 2:10–2:56; YASUMI's bomber→flier→mech 10:50–12:30). For SPEEDHELL's 3-phase boss: phase 1 = one shape, phase 2 = a second shape, phase 3 = both at once — mirroring EPSILON's final REVOLVER phase, which is explicitly a medley of every earlier vocabulary (25:32–25:56). **Partially followed** (3 phases exist); the medley-finale and triple telegraph are the upgrades.

6. **Let the arena itself escalate the boss.** LUNA's last phase turns the whole room red (17:36); EPSILON's sky runs sunset→dusk→night→blood-red→dawn, one sky per phase, so the player reads progress in the horizon (23:14–25:56). SPEEDHELL's 3 boss phases should each restain the starfield (e.g. deep blue → red → white-hot dawn) — this doubles as the cheapest fix to the static-background weakness, since the boss is ~1/4 of the run. **Missing.**

7. **Keep a fixed, non-overlapping color grammar: 4–5 bullet families, items that can't be mistaken for shots, popcorn tuned against the backdrop.** BRDA's red/white rings, gold pills, green shards, magenta orbs, black-dot rings never trade roles across 26 minutes, and its darkest stage (S4 factory, 15:00) runs the brightest enemy/item palette. On SPEEDHELL's dark starfield: light-bodied popcorn, saturated warm bullets, cool green/gold items — and audit every new background tint (principle 1/6) against bullet readability before shipping it. **Likely followed; re-verify per new backdrop tint.**

8. **Put a visible countdown in front of the boss.** S3 and S5 run an on-HUD percent meter (000%→076% at 9:30–10:36; 000%→010% at 21:16–21:44) that climbs as the arena goes abstract — dread the player can read. A one-stage game gets outsized value from this: a "SIGNAL %" ticking through sections 5–7 makes the whole back half feel like an approach. **Missing; cheap and very high-flavor.**

9. **Buy the boss entrance with 6–10 seconds of emptiness.** Every BRDA boss is preceded by the ground literally falling away plus a dialogue card over a bare backdrop (1:56–2:06, 5:12–5:18, 16:00–16:04, 23:00–23:04). SPEEDHELL's release section should END in near-silence — no enemies, backdrop at its most abstract — before phase 1 fires. **Partially followed** (release exists); ensure its last beat is empty, not busy.

10. **Grade the exit.** BRDA closes every stage with the tally card that itemizes BREAK boxes ×8/×16/×32/×64 (2:58, 6:32, 26:04) — the scoring system is taught between stages, not in a manual. A one-stage game should do this once, loudly: an end card that itemizes speed-kill bonuses per section so the player learns what to route next run. **Unknown/likely missing.**
