## Cross-cutting observations

- **Bullet families.** Two-color grammar all loop: PINK/magenta rounds carry the authored patterns (loops 2:40, petals 14:24, needle webs 24:32) and BLUE rounds carry rings/beads/darts (9:28, 13:16, 19:12). Orange needles = fast aimed volleys (2:12). Curtains are always macro-shapes (rings, snakes, blooms) — readable, never noise; big patterns are slow, aimed stuff is fast, and the two are rarely both dense at once.
- **Scale hierarchy.** Popcorn planes/buggies (die in 1 hit, drop nothing much) → midsize tanks/gunships (2–10 laser-seconds, star drops) → LARGE crawlers/battleships/fortresses (3:20, 4:00, 6:12, 8:38 — screen-fraction sprites, slow or static, turrets as sub-targets) → bosses (screen-wide, multi-part). Big things are slow; fast things are small.
- **Item economy.** Kills shower gold stars (fountains at 1:46, 10:24, 14:40, 19:48, 24:56); stars and hidden bees are itemized on every results screen (×50 → ×95 → ×160 → ×195 across the loop) with a No Miss multiplier — the receipt screen is part of the reward loop.
- **Ambiguities.** Exact bee counts per stage are hard to read; stage 2/4 "midboss" designation is by scale not certainty; some HIT readouts are motion-blurred (I report only clearly legible ones); the 4:00 battleship may technically be terrain rather than one enemy entity.

## Principles for an homage

1. **Author the chain as a route, not a stat.** Every DDP wave is placed within link-distance of the next: tank pods 0:56–1:26 build 20→73 HIT, ruins at 11:08–11:16 pay a 120-hit chain (+658,090), and stage 5 is authored end-to-end into a 221-hit chain (~17:00–17:10). For SPEEDHELL: chart the 8 sections as one intended combo line — each wave's spawn time = previous wave's earliest possible kill time + a beat, so a player killing inside the speed-kill window lands on the next wave with the chain alive. Playtest the route, not just the waves.

2. **Popcorn is mortar, big targets are bricks.** Between every tank/hull in DDP there's a string of 1-hit popcorn whose only job is to bridge the chain gap (1:04–1:20, 15:00–15:44, 22:16–22:20). Adopt: in turret alley and the mid gauntlet, lace 3–5 popcorn between elites specifically as chain-fillers; their spawn cadence *is* the chain timer made visible.

3. **Anchor targets to terrain and let terrain change mid-section.** DDP's ground targets sit on ledges, walls, and highways (0:56, 4:00–4:10, 7:48–8:24, 22:16), and every stage swaps terrain 2–3 times mid-scroll (desert→canal→highway 3:10–4:48; clouds→fortress→spires 5:48–8:36). Adopt: give each of SPEEDHELL's 8 sections a visible terrain identity, and mark 2–3 seams where the ground itself changes — it makes one stage feel like a journey (CAVE's stagecraft) at zero mechanical cost.

4. **Ship one enormous set-piece enemy, slow and turreted.** DDP's crawler fortress (3:20–3:36) and grounded battleship (4:00–4:10) own the screen by size and slowness, with turrets as sub-targets that feed the chain. Adopt (scaled to 320×427): one hull ~40–50% of field width in the "turret alley" or "release" section, near-static, 4–6 destructible turrets that chain-link into its core kill. Big = slow; fast = small. Never both.

5. **Ritualize the boss beat: WARNING → entrance → phase ladder → receipt.** Every DDP boss gets a WARNING banner (2:06, 4:48, 8:30, 13:04, 18:04, 23:02), a staged entrance (final boss rises from a silo hatch 23:06–23:14), phases that escalate from fast/aimed into slow/dense (needles 2:12 → loop-curtains 2:40; the final boss saves its pink needle web for the last phase, 24:32–24:52), and an itemized results screen. Adopt all four beats — the ritual is cheap and reads as CAVE instantly.

6. **Keep the chain alive through the boss.** DDP's HIT counter climbs during boss fights via sustained laser contact (45→75 on boss 2, 13→158 on the final boss, 23:16–24:56) — the boss is chain food, not a chain reset. Adopt for SPEEDHELL's 3-phase boss: make each destructible part a speed-kill target that links, so a perfect boss extends the stage combo to the final blow.

7. **Two-color bullet grammar with macro-shapes.** DDP stays dodgeable at high counts because bullets come in exactly two families — pink (authored patterns: loops 2:40, petals 14:24–14:36, webs 24:32) and blue (rings/beads/darts 9:28, 19:12) — and every curtain is a readable shape, not noise. Adopt: two bullet colors with fixed semantics (e.g. pink = pattern/slow, blue = aimed/fast), and build every dense moment from a nameable shape (ring, snake, bloom).

8. **Shower rewards, then hand over a receipt.** Big kills erupt in gold star fountains (1:46, 10:24, 19:48, 24:56) and each stage ends on an itemized bonus screen (stars ×160 = 80,000, bees ×21, No Miss — 20:00–20:10). Adopt lightweight: speed-kills spray score stars along the chain route (the route becomes visible as a trail of gold), and the stage-clear tally itemizes chain peaks/speed-kill count/no-miss.

9. **Identity choice — do NOT adopt slow curtain density as the primary language.** DDP's signature is 2–3 minute stages of slow hypnotic curtains (16:20–16:50 storms, 17:24 bubbles, boss petal storms 14:24, 19:30) that you *wade through* while lasering. That pacing depends on 26-minute loops and anchor-point laser play. SPEEDHELL's identity is Psikyo speed-kill: keep enemy fire fast, aimed, and sparse; permit true curtains only as 5–10 s accents at the midboss and the boss's final phase (mirroring how DDP saves its densest web for the last 20 s, 24:32–24:52).

10. **Identity choice — do NOT adopt CAVE stage length or boss endurance.** DDP back-half stages run ~3 min and bosses 1:35–1:50 (boss 3: 8:38–10:26; boss 5: 18:08–19:52) — endurance is the point. SPEEDHELL's one stage should keep its 8 sections at Psikyo tempo (~20–40 s each) and boss phases at 15–25 s each, with visible part-destruction (principle 6) doing DDP's "structure changes as you win" trick (9:28) on a compressed clock.
