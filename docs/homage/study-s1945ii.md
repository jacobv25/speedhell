# Strikers 1945 II (Psikyo, 1997) — Film Study, Loop 1 of a 2-ALL
Source: 17 contact sheets (`sheets/s1945ii/sheet-01..17.png`), 1 frame / 2s, ~16:58 of footage.
Coverage: full first loop (Missions 1–8), per-mission RESULT screens, and the opening minute of loop 2.

single best ground truth on Psikyo stage length:

Whole loop: ~14 min of play + briefing cards; final boss dies ~15:05; loop 2 starts 15:48 with
visibly added orange suicide/revenge bullets (compare 16:00–16:28 vs 0:10–0:58 on the same stage).
Stage-order note: briefing cards run MISSION 1→8 in sequence in this run; each card shows a photo +
name of the stage's boss ("Huge Flying Boat" 0:06, "Cross Sinker" 8:38) — the boss is telegraphed
before the stage even starts. The first four missions are the game's variable-order block; the frames
can't show the selection mechanism, only the numbered cards.

## Bullet & threat language (cross-stage)
- **Purple/violet needle clusters** — the workhorse: fast AIMED volleys, 3–8 bullets in a tight
  wedge, fired by popcorn, turrets, and midsizes alike (0:32, 1:42, 4:00, 9:00, 10:48). Dodged by
  small pre-emptive strafes, not weaving.
- **Orange/amber round dots** — patterned: rings, fans, arcs, spirals from bosses/midbosses (1:10,
  4:52, 8:26, 13:36, 15:00). Slower, readable, fills space.
- **Red flak bursts** (7:28) and **teal/green diamonds** (13:22, 14:36–14:58) as accent classes.
- Density stays LOW by CAVE standards in every frame — rarely more than ~20–30 bullets on screen —
  but nearly everything is aimed or fast, so lethality is positional, not navigational.
- Score/loot: gold bars sit on terrain and pop out of destroyed structures (4:00, 5:10, 11:36);
  green gem trails near kills; RESULT screen tallies GOLD and SHOOT-DOWN per mission, so speed +
  greed are both scored.

## Speed-kill economy (the core loop)
Frame-visible proof that "kill it before it fires" is the design:
- Popcorn dying at screen-top on entry, 2s into the stage (0:12).
- Midsize burning the moment it settles (4:18); dam-deck gunship destroyed near-instantly (9:38–9:40).
- Boss battleship losing turrets within 6s of arrival (2:42–2:48).
- Player parked point-blank under the M7 walker (12:24–12:44) — max DPS beats max safety.
Every enemy has a fuse; the fuse is the difficulty knob. When the player wins the race, the screen
stays almost empty (see how clean 0:48–0:56 looks); when they lose it, aimed purple wedges arrive.

## Principles for an homage

Aimed at SPEEDHELL (one-stage 320×427 Psikyo-style speed-kill danmaku; 8 sections: popcorn intro →
turret alley → mid gauntlet → midboss → rush → elite pair → release → 3-phase boss).

1. **Budget the stage like Psikyo budgets a mission: 42–80s of stage, then a boss worth 30–50% of
   the total.** Official clear times here run 1'11"–2'11" INCLUDING the boss (RESULT screens,
   15:08–15:46); time-to-boss is 42s (M4) to 80s (M7). SPEEDHELL's ~100s+ single stage before a
   3-phase boss is already at the M7/M8 end of the envelope — that's the right end to imitate for a
   finale-flavored stage, but nothing should push past ~2'15" total or it stops feeling Psikyo.
   Keep the 8 sections at ~10–13s each; M1 changes scenery or threat class roughly every 10s
   (0:10 canyon → 0:22 midsize → 0:36 harbor → 0:48 sea → 0:58 boss).

2. **Give every enemy a fuse, and make the fuse the difficulty.** The visible contract all loop
   long: kill it in its entry window and it never fires (popcorn dead at screen-top 0:12; midsize
   burned on arrival 4:18; dam gunship deleted 9:38–9:40); miss the window and you eat a FAST aimed
   purple wedge (0:32, 1:42, 9:00). SPEEDHELL's speed-kill sections already match this — the check
   is that every wave has an explicit "fires at t+N" beat, and that a full-power player can zero
   most waves. Density low, lethality positional: frames never show CAVE curtains, just 3–8-bullet
   aimed clusters.

3. **Two bullet dialects, strictly cast by role: fast aimed needles for the stage, slow patterned
   dots for bosses.** Purple needle wedges = popcorn/turrets/midsizes (0:32, 4:00, 10:48); orange
   dot rings/fans/arcs/spirals = boss grammar (1:10, 4:52, 8:26, 15:00), with rare accent colors
   (red flak 7:28, teal diamonds 14:36) reserved for special phases. SPEEDHELL should keep this
   caste system: a viewer should identify "stage or boss?" from bullet shape alone. Its 3-phase
   boss earns the dot-pattern dialect; its turret alley must stay in needles.

4. **Bolt the guns onto the landmarks.** Every emplacement in the footage sits ON a background
   feature: turrets on battleship decks (1:36–2:34), guns flanking rail lines (3:36–4:10), AA on
   dam ledges (9:24–9:46), wall pods in corridors (12:52–13:10) — and terrain changes cue wave
   changes (harbor at 0:36, ice shelf at 2:36, dam at 9:24). SPEEDHELL's starfield is the biggest
   deviation from the lineage: a starfield has no ledges. The fix without a full re-theme is
   structure-in-space — derelict hulls, station spines, asteroid rails — scrolled through so the
   turret alley and elite pair are anchored to silhouettes, and each of the 8 sections gets its own
   background "place" the way M6 goes gorge → waterfall → dam face → dam crest → outflow.

5. **Punctuate with midsizes every ~10s, and make the midboss a real boss with its own gimmick.**
   Midsize bombers/gunships arrive on a metronome (0:22, 0:26, 0:46, 4:18, 8:56, 9:38) as
   speed-kill DPS checks. The bigger stages then escalate: M7 runs an elite PAIR midboss
   (spider-mech + white winged mech, 11:16–11:28) and M8's midboss is a golden angel mech with its
   own transformation (13:24–13:46). SPEEDHELL's midboss → rush → elite pair spine matches M7/M8
   exactly — the missing Psikyo touch is giving the midboss and elite pair one theatrical gimmick
   each (a wing-deploy, a claw weapon like the M6 boss's chained anchor at 10:30), not just more HP.

6. **The boss must TRANSFORM, not just phase — telegraph with burn, transform, escalate the
   dialect.** All eight bosses convert vehicle → mecha (flying boat → moth mech 1:00–1:22;
   battleship → walker 2:42–3:26; tank → winged mech 4:28–5:00; tower → crab 8:00–8:34), always
   telegraphed by the current form burning red first (1:06, 3:08, 10:14), and the final boss chains
   4 forms ending in an organic demon and a bare core with a firework desperation (14:00–15:04).
   SPEEDHELL's non-transforming 3-phase boss is its biggest grammar gap: phases should be
   FORM changes (silhouette, movement style, bullet dialect all change), with phase 3 as a
   stripped-down desperation core firing dense radial rings (15:00–15:02 is the template), and each
   handoff marked by the burn-red + explosion beat.

7. **Part-breaks and point-blank invitations.** The M2 boss loses turret groups one at a time with
   individual explosions (2:42–3:08), and the optimal play against the M7 walker is parking
   directly underneath it (12:24–12:44). Bosses strafe/reposition hard (M6 hydrofoil racing on the
   water 9:56–10:10; M7 walker strafing 12:24–12:46), which is what makes point-blanking a skill
   rather than a cheese. SPEEDHELL's boss should have at least one destructible sub-part per phase
   and one attack whose safe spot is CLOSE to the boss, with boss strafing that forces re-earning
   that spot.

8. **Breathe with loot, not with emptiness.** Psikyo's breathers are 6–12s and always paid: the
   gold-bar corridors (rail yard 4:00–4:10, rooftops 5:10–5:20, hangar deck 11:36–11:46) drop
   threat density but keep the player busy harvesting; post-boss briefing cards (0:06, 3:32, 8:38)
   are the only true zero-input rests. SPEEDHELL's "release" section is the analog — it should be
   a medal/loot shower over a landmark, not a blank scroll, and the RESULT-screen idea (per-run
   TIME / GOLD / SHOOT-DOWN, 15:08–15:46) is worth stealing outright since it scores exactly the
   three verbs the game teaches: be fast, be greedy, kill everything.

9. **Telegraph the boss before the stage, and cue it with the landmark.** The briefing card shows
   the boss's photo and name before each mission (0:06 "Huge Flying Boat", 8:38 "Cross Sinker"),
   and inside the stage the boss arrives on a terrain cue (ice shelf edge 2:36→2:40; below-dam
   water 9:48→9:56; bulkhead door 11:48→12:00). SPEEDHELL should open with a one-card target
   briefing naming its boss, and its "release" section should double as the boss's landmark
   approach so section 7 → section 8 reads as arrival, not spawn.

10. **Plan the second loop as a bullet-diff, not new content.** Loop 2 reuses stage 1 verbatim but
    every kill now spits orange revenge dots (compare 16:00–16:12 vs 0:12–0:34) — the speed-kill
    strategy that emptied the screen in loop 1 now FILLS it, inverting the player's own habit.
    For a one-stage game this is the cheapest possible second difficulty: same waves, plus
    on-death aimed dots. It re-monetizes every wave the player has already mastered.

## Ambiguities (not invented)
- M4 boss segmentation (5:48–7:02): the giant bomber, ground fortress, and flying mech are all
  clearly present, but at 2s sampling I can't certify whether the bomber is a midboss or the boss's
  first form, nor the exact number of transforms (≥2, likely 3 configurations).
- Mission 2 and Mission 7 briefing cards fall in blank/dark frames (~1:26, ~10:38); inferred from
  the pattern, not observed.
- Small green diamonds near kills are read as score gems and teal diamonds in M8 as bullets; the
  sampling rate can't fully separate item sparkle from projectile in every frame.
- Power-up carrier waves (the red/white item planes) are not individually resolvable at 2s/frame;
  item economy is described from the gold bars and RESULT tallies, which are unambiguous.
