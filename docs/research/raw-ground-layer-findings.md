# Destructible environment & ground targets in classic Japanese shmups — cited findings

Scope: Psikyo, CAVE, Raizing, Toaplan, Hudson/Compile, Seibu, Taito (RayForce), Konami (TwinBee). Method: 22 WebSearch queries; ~45 pages fetched; quotes below were read in the fetched page unless marked [snippet only]. shmups.system11.org, tvtropes, fandom and gamefaqs were unreachable (Cloudflare / 402 / archive.today rate-limit, Internet Archive offline during the session), so forum-derived claims are flagged.

---

## Q1. Who has destructible scenery, what it pays, whether it fires

**Raizing — Battle Garegga.** The most explicit "terrain is a score layer" design in the set. shmups.wiki: "There are many background elements throughout the stages that may be destroyed by the player's special weapon. These reveal many secrets, including hidden items (often large medal caches), extra points, and other targets such as extra enemies or the infamous pink flamingos." [1] Stage page specifics (all [2]): Stage 2 — "If the tanks are destroyed while crushing a house, they will drop a medal. Otherwise, they will drop a small weapon fragment." / "If a house is destroyed by the player's special weapon before a tank reaches it, it will uncover an option power-up." Stage 3 — medal track: "each segment destroyed will reveal four medals"; "Each of the six water silos will reveal a medal when destroyed by a special weapon." Stage 4 — "The large pipe ... is worth 10,000 points and always drops an option power-up"; "The four silos ... are worth 10,000 points each"; five pipes "Each one is worth 10,000 points and reveals two medals"; dock rails "each release 7-8 medals." Stage 6 — tank hatches "uncover two medals each. They also stop spawning tanks when destroyed"; laser turrets "5,000 points when destroyed normally and 50,000 points when destroyed by a special weapon." Stage 7 — rear thrusters "award 50,000 points each." Ground military units pay resources rather than score: "Small Weapon Fragment ... Dropped by grounded enemies such as tanks or turrets" [1]. Do they fire? Tanks/turrets yes (they are enemies); silos, pipes, houses, rails, hatches are inert scenery that only pays. Note the split: scenery is mostly bomb-gated ("special weapon"), which turns bombs into a scoring resource — Retro XP: "bombs are how you find hidden caches of point chips, how you do things like unveil hidden environmental bits like the flock of flamingos on the second stage" [3].

**Raizing — Armed Police Batrider.** shmups.wiki enemy list (154 entries across 7 areas) is a straight table of scenery and enemies with shot/aura/bomb values. Inert urban scenery pays, and pays 10x when killed by aura or bomb: "Cars" 100 / 1000 / 1000; "Fire hydrant" 3000 / 30000 / 30000; "Hut" 5000 / 50000 / 50000; "Large Billboard" 3000 / 30000 / "30000 per hit"; "Desk" 30000; "Monitor" 50000 [4]. Vehicles pay conditionally: "Patrol car ... Medal if destroyed by boss, otherwise none"; "Armored Vehicle ... 4 Bomb fragments while moving, Otherwise an Option/Large Shot" [4]. A forum ST claims medals are "about 40 to 50%" of score [snippet only, 5].

**Seibu — Raiden / Raiden II.** HG101: "Each stage bonus is based upon medals, bonus items you collect throughout each stage by destroying crates, buildings, and other ground targets. Medals are five hundred points on base, with each one also adding one thousand points to your stage bonus, and your score is further multiplied by your bombs." [6] shmups.wiki confirms the formula "1,000 points * Bomb stock * Collected medals" and "Miclus 3,000 points" (HG101 says Miclus 10,000 / Fairy 30,000 — contradiction, see below) [7][6]. Raiden II: "five-hundred point medals which, as before, are uncovered by tearing up the scenery"; super medals "worth three thousand points on base" [8]; shmups.wiki: "Medals are found in predetermined spots throughout the stages. They can be gold, blue or a Miclus"; "A fairy can be found in stages 1 and 4 by shooting a specific tree"; Stage 4: "Destroying the circle of rocks will reveal the tree"; bonus "((Bomb stock * Gold medals) + Blue medals) * 1,000" [9]. 1cclog Raiden II: "Released by little ground buildings, missiles can be straight nukes (M) or homing (H)" [10] — i.e. weapon items also come out of ground structures. Firing: buildings/crates don't; tanks do ("barrages of tanks coming from below" [10]; "enemy tanks and battleships fire quick star-shaped projectiles" [8]).

**Psikyo — Strikers 1945 / Gunbird.** HG101: "Gold bars are found inside destructible buildings or ground-based enemies, and are very common in most levels." [11] shmups.wiki: "Gold is worth 200, 500, 1,000 or 2,000pts depending on what frame of animation it is on" and gold is "Dropped by grounded enemies and objects"; "the player wants to collect the gold bars on the frame that they flash white, as this will always reward the player with 2000pts" [12]. Gunbird: "Land enemies and buildings can be blown up and will sometimes have gold underneath. The gold will cycle from shiny to dull and you'll get more points if you pick it up when it's shiny." [13] Gunbird 2 coins: "200, 500, 1000 or 2000 points, depending on when they are collected during their spin animation"; chain bonus 500/1000/2000 for consecutive 2000pt coins [14]. Strikers battleships are multi-part ground targets: Stages page — "the first two you have to disarm, while the third will move and it's bridge is destroyable"; "nine moving vehicles. The tank destroyers will only attack" (i.e. some ground vehicles are inert); Gunbird train "each of its three parts is worth 8,000 pts" [15]. Fandom claims the Ise-class has "four turrets and a flight deck" and back turrets should be killed "before the flight deck for extra gold bars" [snippet only, 16]. Firing: tanks/turrets/battleship guns yes; buildings/trucks no.

**CAVE — DoDonPachi.** Chain system explicitly counts environment: GPS "emphasizes scoring by performing hit/kill chains upon enemies and the environment" [17]. HG101: "A new method introduced in Dodonpachi is the ability to 'stall' the bar by hitting large enemies or buildings with the Laser." [18] Environment easter eggs: Stage 1-2/2-2 "large bunker turret" reveals flowers worth "10pts every frame"; Stage 1-4/2-4 "'destroyed' Toaplan logo" hides flowers [17]. DaiFukkatsu: "You can access the hidden route during stage 1, by destroying three specific silos before they are run over by the tanks at the beginning of the stage" [19]; Stage 1-3 "The main source for combo value in the stage are the big yellow and blue ships with multiple turrets"; Stage 1-5 "full of tanks and other ground objects that show up only briefly so getting all the possible kills here is challenging" [20]. ESP Ra.De.: ground troopers and tanks are enemies ("later on, smaller units and tanks appear") but the wiki has no scenery-scoring [21]. Ketsui: "Destroying all pieces of the midboss battleship before killing it will grant a 1up item" [22]. DaiOuJou wiki: laser on a large enemy "will hold the meter steady and slowly accumulate hits" [23]; no scenery scoring found.

**Toaplan.** Tiger-Heli: "You fight against air and ground-based enemies (planes and tanks, respectively) ... your main weapon can hit both types of targets"; "For no reason other than to be a jerk, you can blow up people's houses here." [24] Truxton: "colored levers throughout the game, which will grant you an extra life if you destroy them with the same colored weapon" [25]; shmups.wiki Tatsujin: extends require "a specific weapon to shoot a specific ground target: green in stage 1, red in stage 2, blue in stage 5" [26]. Batsugun: "All ships can level up by gaining experience points by shooting down enemies, bosses, or parts of the stage environment"; pigs "are revealed upon destroy particular enemies or parts of the background" [27]. Kyuukyoku Tiger: "Scoring ... is extremely basic, with each enemy and item offering a flat amount of points"; 3000 per P-Star [28]. Fire Shark: "Every so often, your plane flies over a generic enemy base" [29]. Uemura on ground enemies firing: "In Kyuukyoku Tiger, when you got close to a ground enemy they wouldn't fire at you ... we wanted as much as possible to avoid the situation where the player didn't know what killed him." [30]

**Hudson / Compile.** Star Soldier's ground panels are the purest case: StrategyWiki — "groups of tiles that can be shot and destroyed for extra points. Both the star tile and the snake tile are destroyed in one shot, and are worth 100 points"; Dragon 200, Phoenix 300; skull tile 400 ("Total shots 9 7 5 3"); eyeball tiles "24 shots ... 2000 points ... destroy both eyes at the same time ... 80,000 point bonus"; "tiles with a large P" release power-ups [31]. Secret tiles: "In addition to the six secret ZEGU tiles that you can collect for bonus points, each stage has at least one extra type of secret tile" — Milon/Whale 40,000, Warp, 1-UP, Nozawa 2,000,000 [32]. 1cclog: "six hidden X tiles in each stage that can be easily uncovered and destroyed while increasing in value, and if you find them all the last one will be worth 80.000 points" [33]. The 500/1,000/4,000/10,000/40,000/80,000 = 135,500 sequence appeared only in a search snippet [snippet only, 34]. None of these fire. Soldier Blade dev: "we took the letters 'ZG' from the hidden point panel 'zegu' in Star Soldier" [35]. Aleste/Power Strike: "destroy as many enemies and ground targets as possible"; "weapons 4,5, and 8 do not hit ground targets" [36]. Blazing Lazers / kontek HG101 Star Soldier: "These blocks give you points, and is one of the keys to getting high scores" [37].

**Taito — RayForce.** Whole ground layer is a separate target class: "The Lock-on Laser ... is used to destroy or damage enemies in the background layer"; multiplier x1, x2, x4 ... x128 across 8 locks [38]. HG101: lasers "mostly reserved for ground units or enemy aircraft floating several meters below you"; "asteroids that break off of large rocks, giant ships being torn apart (sometimes by you), collapsing towers" [39].

**Konami — TwinBee.** Xevious-style split: "Ground/Miracle Bells, Stars, and Fruit appear by destroying ground enemies"; "Fruit — Ground item worth 50, 100, or 200 points" [40]; HG101: "Instead of turrets on the ground, there are pine cones." [41] Ground layer pays items, air layer (clouds→bells) pays score.

## Q2. Score / routing role

- **DDP chain fillers.** Environment counts for GPS ("enemies and the environment") and lasering "large enemies or buildings" stalls the meter [17][18]; DFK's stage 1-5 tanks "show up only briefly" so ground kills are the hard part of a full-kill route [20]; DFK ura route is opened by killing three silos before tanks crush them [19]. Ikeda's stated reason for lenient chaining: "we made the chaining more lenient compared with Donpachi, so that players like me with no interest in scoring could be able to enjoy it too" [42]. No source gave a stage-level count of ground fillers in DDP.
- **Psikyo gold.** All Strikers/Gunbird gold comes "from selected ground enemies" [43] / "destructible buildings or ground-based enemies" [11]; 2000 vs 200 by timing [43][12]; Gunbird 2 adds chain bonuses [14]. 1cclog Strikers: "Gold bars and extra items are the main source of score boost ... One of them is to destroy all parts of a boss and milk its destructible projectiles." [43]
- **Garegga medals.** 100→10,000 chain, reset on a miss [1]; scenery caches are the payout: "If you can reveal a hidden point chip cache with six or eight or however many 10,000 point chips because your streak is maxed out ... that's a huge boost" [3]; strategy page: "If you find yourself rebuilding a medal chain, make sure not to uncover any medal caches until you reach 500-point medals" [44]. The "dropped chain costs 129,500 points" figure is [snippet only, 45]. Route-relevant scenery numbers in [2] above (e.g. 24 medals from three gunblimps in Stage 6).
- **Raiden.** Medals only from ground destruction, and worth 1,000 × bombs at stage end [6][7]; "you only get the points for the medals you acquire in one life" in Raiden 1 [6], fixed in II [8]; hidden fairy/Miclus under trees/rocks [9].
- **Star Soldier.** Tile values as above; Takahashi Meijin: "So we had scoring tricks too, but they weren't essential to the game… it was just 'if you want to score high, you'll need to do this.'" [46]
- **Toaplan.** Truxton ground-target extends [25][26]; Batsugun environment gives XP [27]; no "destroy the base" bonus found in any fetched source — unverified.

## Q3. Fraction of shootable objects that are ground/scenery

No published stage-by-stage census was found for any game. Best available:

- **Batrider enemy list (my count from the fetched table, 154 entries across 7 areas):** ~30 entries are inert scenery (cars, trash bins, hydrant, signs, billboard, hut, station wagon, patrol car, containers, airport glass, countertop, flower pot, bench, gas tanks, vent, desk, monitor, covers, capsules) ≈ 19% of entry types; ground military (tanks, turrets, batteries, cannons, bikers, vehicles) is roughly another 45–50%; pure air units (helicopters, aircraft, zako, missiles, birds) ≈ 20–25%. This counts types, not spawn instances [4].
- **Garegga:** wiki flags that "In Stage 3, Stage 5, and Stage 6, grounded popcorn enemies appear which are guaranteed to drop items" versus air popcorn dropping every fifth kill [1] — ground popcorn is a distinct, item-rich class.
- **Strikers 1945 Plus review:** "The screen is littered with planes, tanks, cannons, and eight times as many bullets." [47] (qualitative)
- **Raiden forum characterization** of "sniping tanks, fixed sprayer turrets, slowly advancing but persistently shooting heavy jets, and scads of 1shottable but screamingly fast kamikazes" [snippet only, 48].

Thin evidence: fraction question is essentially unanswered by the literature.

## Q4. Developer commentary on why the ground layer matters

- **Toaplan (Yuge, STG Gameside):** asked "Toaplan's placement of ground and air enemies is really superb. Did that take a lot of time in development?" — "I'd say it used up roughly 1/3 of our time, maybe. We started with the question 'what will players think of this?'"; "We'd want bullets to be fired at this point, so let's place cannons on the enemy here… we had lots of conversations like that." [49]
- **Toaplan (Yuge, Chronicle Q&A):** Kyuukyoku Tiger — "Naturally, STGs are about the thrill of blowing stuff up"; Same! Same! Same! — "For that Toaplan thrill, we added places where you could burn everything up with the super-flashy flamethrower"; on animated background elements: "Our designer Nakaoka drew that as part of the background, and I said 'since you've already done this much, let's make them move!'" [50]
- **Toaplan (Uemura):** ground enemies suppress fire at close range so players know what killed them [30].
- **Seibu (Komazawa):** Raiden theme "realism" — "The way enemy ships fly, the way tanks fire, and so on… every enemy and character in that game had a backstory"; heavy iteration on explosion frames ("after the first 3 animation frames, put a momentary pause") [51]; "the screen would get hectic and covered without bullets despite not being a bullet-hell"; "all our STGs feel like they take place in real environments"; medals added to Viper Phase 1 NEW "since the American market seemed to like collecting items" [52].
- **CAVE (Ikeda/Kouyama):** lenient chaining, HIT counter "big numbers ... make you go 'whoaa!!'" [42]; Ikeda: "the most important things are getting the difficulty balance correct, the enemy placement, and the right balance for the danmaku patterns" [42].
- **Hudson:** Zegu→Zeograd naming [35]; Takahashi on optional scoring tricks [46]. No Nozawa interview on panels found.
- **Raizing/Yagawa:** no medal/scenery design commentary found on shmuplations (Ibara interview has only 6 short Yagawa answers, one wanting players to enjoy "the destruction" [53]); T3-Kamui player interview has none [54]. Unverified for Yagawa.
- **boghog (Shmup 101):** "Ceasefire zone ... primarily used for ground targets such as turrets and tanks"; "Bullet sealing ... weaker ground enemies don't shoot if the player's sitting on top of them"; "Adding some interaction between the enemy layer and the background layer helps make your level feel like an actual physical space. This can be achieved with destructible scenery, hidden bonuses and various polish effects like ground explosions leaving behind craters." [55]
- Mark MSX / STG Weekly: no ground-layer-specific writeup located.

## Q5. The feel argument

- Density/collateral reads as spectacle: HG101 RayForce — "there's an impressive amount of crap that goes on one hundred meters below you" [39]; Raiden II — "Shrapnel and debris fly all over the place, and you can even see pieces of enemy aircraft falling to the ground" [10]; Garegga — "each enemy goes down in different blazes of glory" [56]; Tiger-Heli — houses you can blow up for no reason [24].
- Hull-stripping as a designed pastime: Garegga Stage 5 "Nearly every inch of Slayer may be destroyed using a special weapon for bonus points"; core shrapnel "gives lots of points (on the order of a few hundred thousand)" [2]; Strikers 1cclog "destroy all parts of a boss and milk its destructible projectiles" [43]; Ketsui full-part battleship kill = 1up [22]; DFK multi-turret ships are "the main source for combo value" [20]; Batrider "Large Tank ... Large Bomb if all parts are scrapped" [4].
- Toaplan's own framing: "the thrill of blowing stuff up" / "burn everything up" [50]; 1/3 of dev time on ground+air placement [49].
- boghog's "physical space" rationale [55] is the clearest explicit design statement that scenery interaction is what makes a level read as a place.

## Contradictions / thin evidence

- Raiden Miclus value: shmups.wiki says 3,000 [7]; HG101 says 10,000 (Fairy 30,000) [6]; StrategyWiki says 10,000 [57]. Raiden II wiki says fairy 10,000 [9]. Version differences likely.
- Strikers 1945 II shmups.wiki calls the pickups "medals ... 200–2,000 based on proximity" not gold and does not attribute them to ground objects [58]; Strikers 1 wiki attributes gold to "grounded enemies and objects" [12].
- Star Soldier Zegu sequence and 135,500 total: snippet only [34]; StrategyWiki lists "six secret ZEGU tiles" without values [32]; 1cclog confirms six and 80,000 last [33]. Search snippet also said "about 8 ZEGs" — conflict, unverified.
- Garegga "129,500 dropped-chain cost" and Batrider "40–50%": snippet only [45][5].
- Toaplan "destroy the ground base bonus": not found in any fetched source — unverified.
- Yagawa design commentary on medals/scenery: not found.
- Ground/air fraction: no published counts; Batrider figure is my own type-count from a wiki table.
- shmups.system11.org forum quotes (Raiden ground/air interplay; "tank placement in Toaplan games") could not be fetched; snippet only [48].
