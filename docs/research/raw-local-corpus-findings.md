## boghog, SHMUP WORKSHOP 04 "Enemy Design & Effects" (2023-01-23) — transcript ~/Dev/boghog-research/transcripts
Verbatim (auto-caption punctuation restored):
- "Keeping the pace up: try to keep enemy health as low as possible. Think about what sort of function they will need to fulfill in encounters and give them just enough health to fulfill it. This makes the player feel more powerful and keeps the game moving."
- "Safely approaching enemies shouldn't be disproportionately dangerous. Don't encourage players to ignore enemies. Bulky enemies that move downwards often suffer from this problem because they block up more and more of the screen over time."
- **"Bullet ceiling is a nice anti-frustration mechanic where ground enemies will stop shooting if the player is close to them. Sometimes air enemies can be sealed too, but this isn't as common. Most games have a zone at the bottom of the screen where enemies will stop shooting, since the player typically can't shoot backwards — this prevents a lot of needless frustration. A lot of games have a dead zone at the top of the screen where enemies cannot be damaged; this prevents players from shooting enemies before they appear on screen."**
- "Enemies should react to getting hit... visible damage and destructible parts sell the feeling of interaction even more. The shmup objectively cannot be good if it doesn't have good explosions."

Interpretation for SPEEDHELL: the canon "seal" is (a) ground enemies only, at close range — a reward for point-blanking that specific enemy; (b) a BOTTOM dead zone for enemies that have passed below the player. SPEEDHELL's `mayFire` seals EVERY enemy (air, boss, elites, mids) whenever the player is within 40px vertically or above them — that generalizes a ground-only rule to the whole game.

## boghog, SHMUP WORKSHOP 05 "Level Design" (2023-02-06)
- **Why the top is dangerous in canon (physics, not silence):** "Players will tend to stay away from the top of the screen for several reasons: that's where most enemies will be — even if they don't shoot many bullets, their collision hitboxes still make that area very dangerous; basic straightforward shots limit the player's offense if they're not directly below the enemies; being close to enemies gives the player very little time to react to threats. Players will stay close to the bottom center of the screen since this gives them good DPS, enough time to react to threats and a lot of screen space to work with. Players will get close to bulky enemies to do extra damage whenever they get the opportunity, then they'll back away when the enemy starts to shoot. As long as popcorn enemies keep spawning in, players will be forced to move side to side to stream bullets and minimize danger."
- **The Top Line pattern:** "invaluable for creating flow within levels; it exists everywhere but Toaplan is the clearest and most blatant example. Break the top of the screen into a bunch of lanes — around 5 or 7 tends to be the norm... keep the player mobile by either spawning enemies on opposite sides of the screen or keeping one lane's worth of distance between each spawn. Higher HP enemies tend to require larger gaps than weaker enemies... don't spawn two or more stronger enemies at the same time... avoid clustering enemies on one side of the screen or stacking turrets or tanks vertically."
- **Wave overlap:** "Shmup levels are timelines. Putting enemy spawns close together on the timeline creates overlap... if they kill wave number one quickly they'll have more time to reposition and less lingering bullets... Heavy overlap puts a lot of pressure on players and forces them to move around very aggressively... Overlapping waves of lower HP enemies give the players more routing freedom and shift focus on dodge while still preserving very high intensity."
- **Tanks and popcorn:** "Sequences of elite enemy spawns do a good job of creating a main route through the level, so they can get boring if not supported by smaller enemies like flying popcorn or ground tanks and turrets. Smaller enemies make everything more dynamic, so layering them around your main spawns will keep the players on their toes."
- **Section themes incl. destructible terrain:** "Some sections might focus on popcorn rushes, some on ground turrets, some sections can have a lot of destructible terrain mixed with the enemies, some can focus on getting just-in-time bullet cancels, and some can be extremely tight gauntlets of difficult enemies."
- **Environments / anchors:** "Memorizing dense chaotic enemy spawns can be very difficult. Environments alleviate this by connecting the enemy spawns to memorable landmarks... Foreground/background interaction is key: if enemies spawn from ground hatches which are baked into the surrounding scenery it will be easier to simplify and remember their spawn points. Destructible background objects also serve as anchor points that action happens around... Non-intrusive set pieces are also great for breaking up parts of your level, especially if enemies are tied to them in some way."

## boghog + Danbo, "How to Avoid the EuroShmup" (2021-04-03)
- "If you have a shmup [that's] euro and you're trying to make it a little bit more Japanese, one thing you could maybe do is drop down the enemy health and then increase the enemy count. So instead of having a few high-HP enemies that are just big blocks you avoid, break that up into a bunch more small-to-medium HP enemies that attack the screen from different angles and give the player much more to think about."
- "Give enemies the minimum amount of health... as long as they get to do the thing that you want them to do. You can also cheat: you can give the enemy an invincibility phase when they initially scroll on screen, and then have them shoot, and then remove the invincibility. That way... make sure they shoot no matter what." (← canonical version of SPEEDHELL's r17 arrival shot: armor-until-first-shot.)
- On bullet taxonomy: suicide bullets are the "random junk / just get paid" class, not micro-dodge content.

## Mark MSX, "10 Modern Game Design Trends that Hurt Longevity" (2024-06-20)
- "A lot of modern games... do not have entropy: they do not have consequence for the player being idle or not doing anything... there needs to be consequence for an action; something needs to be happening at all times even if the player isn't doing anything." (Majora's Mask timer as the example.)

## Mark MSX, "Top 10 Greatest Shmup Developers" (2024-04-13)
- Psikyo: "not exactly a bullet hell, but not old school either... I like to call it speed hell... they really emphasize memorization, but through reading the patterns rather than just things appearing on screen."
- RayForce (Taito): "you can only target and shoot enemies in the background and then as they come into the foreground you have to use your regular shot" — background layer as targets.
