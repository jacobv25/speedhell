# Wrapper genres for a bullet-hell payload: cited findings

*Deep research 2026-08-30 for GAME #2 (the Trojan shmup: story/RPG/progression
wrapper as the attraction, bullet-hell payload). Run under a no-WebSearch
constraint (session budget exhausted) by direct navigation of the canonical
sources — Zukowski/howtomarketagame, GameDiscoverCo, Wikipedia, Steam store
pages; the constraint's blind spots are listed under Contradictions. [fetched &
verified] = number/quote confirmed in fetched text.*


**Question:** For a small indie team's game #2 — attraction = story/RPG/progression systems, core action payload = bullet-hell/shmup (the Undertale trick, reversed) — which wrapper genre is most lucrative and attractive on today's market?

---

## 1. Genre economics on Steam for small teams

**Zukowski's 2022 genre study** ("What genres are popular on Steam in 2022," Apr 18 2022) analyzed 32,202 indie games released 2019–2022 (18,563 with >10 reviews), using VG Insights data and a customized Boxleiter method. Ranked by **median earnings** [fetched & verified]:

- **Top:** Roguelike Deckbuilder (highest median, and only 99 games released in the window — high demand, low supply), 4X strategy, builder/construction, survival craft, visual novels.
- **Bottom:** 2D platformers, 3D platformers, VR, FPS, **shoot-em-ups**, tower defense. **Straight shmups are explicitly in his lowest-earning tier.**
- Exact dollar figures per genre are in charts, not text; the fetched page gave rankings only [fetched & verified as rankings, not dollar values].

**Companion post** ("More evidence…," May 30 2022): counting appearances in Steam's monthly Top 20 releases over 6 months — **RPG 25, Simulation 19, Adventure 17, Action 15, FPS 13, Rogue 12, Strategy 12, Platformer 6, Puzzle 5**. "RPG, Building, Rogue, Strategy, Simulation" dominate; platformers that do well "were paired with Metroidvania and Rogues which add that deep complexity" [fetched & verified].

**Key nuance for this project:** story-RPG ranks near the top of what Steam buyers want (25/top-20 appearances, the single highest), while the arcade/shmup presentation of the *same* action ranks near the bottom. Zukowski's "Crafty Buildy" framework (Dec 2024) is blunter: action/RPG/arcade are "Going Around" (content-based) games, which he says face harder odds than systems-based games — but his Strategy bucket includes **roguelites, deckbuilders, autobattlers**, i.e., systems-y wrappers that can host action [fetched & verified].

**GameDiscoverCo** ("How have the genres of hit PC games changed since 2021?", Aug 2026; games >50k copies sold, by top-5 Steam tags): **Roguelite up 39 spots to #1, Roguelike up 37 to #2, Life Sim up 31 to #6, Idler up 46 to #15**; decliners: **JRPG −24, Turn-Based Strategy −19, RTS −17, Survival Horror −17**. Decline attributed to "more hybrid-genre, roguelite-y games." Median hit price fell $19.99 → $16.99 [fetched & verified].

**Horror** (Zukowski, "14 Reasons…," Oct 2 2023): 2022 data — 1,341 horror releases, 88 reached 1,000+ reviews (**6.5% "hit" rate**) vs platformers 1,428 releases, 32 hits (**2.2%**). Horror median revenue $1,200 overall, but **$13,271 median for horror priced >$9.99** vs $5,810 for platformers >$9.99 [fetched & verified].

**Q1 2026 check** (Zukowski, May 2026): consistent winners are adventure, simulation, idle, friendslop; deckbuilders spiked (3 hits vs the usual 1/yr — incl. Slay the Spire 2); 2 survivors-likes broke through; horror dropped 7→3 hits; zero 2D platformers. "Genres don't really change that much" year to year [fetched & verified].

## 2. Wrapper candidates, with market evidence

### (a) Roguelite action — **strong existence proofs at every team size**
- **Hades** (Supergiant, ~20 people): 700K in EA + 1M+ total in first week of 1.0 (Sept 2020); BAFTA Best Game, DICE GOTY, first Hugo for a game; Metacritic 93 [fetched & verified, Wikipedia].
- **Enter the Gungeon** (Dodge Roll, 4 founders): literally "a 2016 **bullet hell roguelike**"; 200K first week, 800K by Jan 2017, **3M+ by Jan 2020** [fetched & verified, Wikipedia].
- **Nova Drift** (solo dev Jeffrey "Chimeric" Nielson): **396,218 lifetime units** as of Aug 2024; started with 4,000 wishlists; tagged Arena Shooter / Bullet Hell / Shoot 'Em Up; 96% positive of 9,083 Steam reviews; 61.4% of its players also own Vampire Survivors [fetched & verified, Zukowski Sep 2024 + Steam]. Zukowski's framing: it succeeds as an "**arcade roguelike**" — arcade simplicity + deep upgrade systems.
- GDCo: roguelite is the **#1 tag among >50k-copy hits in 2025** [fetched & verified].

### (b) Survivors-like / bullet heaven — **proven demand, now a craft-gated market**
- **Vampire Survivors** (initially solo, ~£1,100 asset budget): dev's own store copy is "lets you **be the bullet hell**"; 98% of 127,668 Steam reviews positive; Galante estimated at **£40M by Aug 2024** (Sunday Times via Wikipedia); BAFTA Best Game 2023 [fetched & verified].
- **Deep Rock Galactic: Survivor**: 1M copies in ~1 month (Feb–Mar 2024), peak CCU 56,943 (second only to VS's 77,061), median playtime >10h — but 76% of owners already owned DRG (IP carried it) [fetched & verified, Zukowski].
- **Saturation:** Zukowski's "cycle of a hit genre" (Nov 2025): by May 2025 **three games (VS, Brotato, DRG:S) held >75% of the VS-like market**; the genre is in Phase 4 (big-IP entrants: DRG:S, TemTem Swarm, Warhammer Survivors) [fetched & verified]. His "2026 State of Bullet Heavens" (May 2026): Valve made "Bullet Heaven" an official tag; solo dev Vital Shell still broke through (17,826 launch wishlists, 1,500+ Overwhelmingly Positive reviews, a VS bundle deal) — but via a distinctive aesthetic niche (mecha × PSX retro), and Zukowski **explicitly advises first-timers away from the genre now**: "the days of rapid-release profitability have ended" [fetched & verified].

### (c) Narrative RPG à la Undertale/Everhood — **highest ceiling, thinnest middle**
- **Undertale** (Toby Fox, near-solo, 32 months): combat is explicitly "similar to a bullet hell shooter"; 530,343 Steam copies by end-2015, 1M by Feb 2016, 3.5M Steam players by 2018, Steam Spy 5–10M by Dec 2025; **155,978 Steam reviews, 96% positive** at $9.99 [fetched & verified].
- **Everhood** (2-person Foreign Gnomes): "adventure RPG" with rhythm/bullet-hell lane combat, called the "anti-Undertale"; **8,184 Steam reviews (English-filtered), 95% Overwhelmingly Positive** at $9.99 — so the caller's "~5K reviews" intuition was right in magnitude: Everhood is ~5% of Undertale's review scale, but by Boxleiter logic ~8K reviews × ~30–50 × $9.99 still implies a very healthy 2-person outcome (mid six to seven figures gross, estimate — [unverified inference]). Sequel shipped Mar 2025 [fetched & verified except revenue inference].
- Market context: GDCo shows **JRPG down 24 spots** among hits since 2021 [fetched & verified] — the wrapper that works here is "quirky story-rich indie RPG" (Undertale's tags: Story Rich, Choices Matter, Multiple Endings, Bullet Hell), not traditional JRPG structure.

### (d) Roguelike deckbuilder hybrid — **best economics per Zukowski, no shooter hybrid proof**
- Zukowski 2022: **highest median earnings of any genre, only 99 games released 2019–2022** [fetched & verified]. Still viable per his 2025 buyers guide ("Yes I think they are still viable, no I don't think too many are made"), with a warning against straight StS clones [fetched & verified].
- **Slay the Spire** (2 leads at MegaCrit): 1M by Jun 2018, 1.5M by Mar 2019 [fetched & verified]. **Balatro** (solo, 2.5 years): $1M gross in 8 hours, 1M copies in a month, **5M+ by Jan 2025**; GDC Awards GOTY; 98% of 103,890 Steam reviews [fetched & verified].
- **Deckbuilder × shmup existence proof: none found** in any fetched source. This is genuinely unclaimed territory (with corresponding design risk — card-pacing vs real-time dodging is an unsolved marriage). [thin evidence — absence, not disproof]

### (e) Monster collector / creature RPG
- **Cassette Beasts** (Bytten Studio, **2 full-time devs** + part-timers, Godot): Metacritic 83, "extremely positive" Steam feedback, console + mobile ports, explicitly targeted adults who outgrew Pokémon [fetched & verified; no sales figures available]. Proves 2-person feasibility; combat there is turn-based, not action.
- No Zukowski/GDCo market-size data on the tag was reachable without search [unverified/thin]. GDCo notes TemTem Swarm exists — a creature-IP **survivors-like**, i.e., the hybrid this question imagines already appeared as a Phase-4 product [fetched & verified mention only].

### (f) Horror
- Zukowski's numbers above: 6.5% hit rate, $13,271 median for >$9.99 horror, "high jank tolerance," fans accept 30–60-minute games, capsules are cheap, competition skill level low [fetched & verified]. He literally recommends horror as the genre where **execution speed beats concept novelty** for new devs (Vital Shell post) [fetched & verified].
- **Horror × shmup existence proof: none found** in fetched sources. But note horror is a *mood*, not a mechanic — Undertale itself carries "Psychological Horror" and "Dark" tags [fetched & verified], and low-fi pixel horror (FAITH: The Unholy Trinity — which has bullet-hell-adjacent chase/dodge segments — appears in Zukowski's "secretly popular sub-genres" list [fetched & verified as listed; its mechanics characterization is my knowledge, unverified]).

### (g) Rhythm / music
- **Just Shapes & Beats** (Berzerk Studio, ~13–14 credited): "music-based bullet hell," Metacritic 83, **97% of 13,418 Steam reviews** at $19.99 list, still selling 8 years post-launch [fetched & verified; no unit sales published].
- **Everhood** (above) fuses rhythm + narrative RPG + bullet hell at 2-person scale. Rhythm is a proven *coating* for bullet hell but the two fetched proofs top out in the ~10–15K review band — an order of magnitude below the RPG and deckbuilder ceilings [fetched & verified].

### (h) Cozy / progression sim ("Stardew with combat")
- Zukowski's site returned **no dedicated cozy/farming market-data post** via on-site search (closest: horse-game niche piece, and the Crafty Buildy guide which lists farming under "Buildy") [fetched & verified as absence]. GDCo: **Life Sim up 31 spots to #6** among >50k-copy hits [fetched & verified]. But no existence proof of cozy-sim × bullet hell was found, and the audience mismatch is the obvious risk (the wrapper attracts players who least want the payload). [thin evidence]

## 3. Meta-progression: what the data-adjacent commentary says

- **Zukowski on VS (Jan 2022):** the gap VS filled was "cheap really quick dose adrenaline-inducing games" **with deep meta-progression** — his "Hyper Hardcore" framing: stripped-down tutorialization, total concentration, but a progression system that always pays out [fetched & verified].
- **Zukowski on DRG:S (Apr 2024):** successful survivors-likes pair "simple controls" with "**deep**" meta-games — this reflects "typical Steam player preferences," not casual dumbing-down [fetched & verified].
- **Zukowski on genre cycles (Nov 2025):** second-wave entrants can *define* a genre by adding one mechanic — his named example is **Rogue Genesia's meta progression** influencing the whole VS-like wave, and 20 Minutes Till Dawn's click-to-fire [fetched & verified].
- **Hades:** God Mode exists so struggling players "progress more easily and experience the full story" — the canonical proof that a difficulty-forward action game widens its audience through story + guaranteed progression [fetched & verified, Wikipedia].
- **Counter-signal:** Vital Shell's dev deliberately *rejected* grindy meta-currencies to "respect player time" and still hit Gold-tier wishlists [fetched & verified] — meta-progression must feel like build-unlocking (Nova Drift's 200+ modular upgrades), not chore-grind. The tension with arcade purity is real but the market has voted: every >1M-unit case in this document (VS, Hades, Gungeon, Balatro, DRG:S) has run-to-run permanent progression; the pure-arcade entrant (straight shmup tag) sits in Zukowski's bottom revenue tier.

## 4. Team-size reality check

- Solo/near-solo proofs at large scale: **Vampire Survivors** (solo, £1,100 assets, ~1 year to EA), **Balatro** (solo, 2.5 yrs), **Undertale** (near-solo, 32 months), **Nova Drift** (solo — but 10 years total, 5 in EA), **Vital Shell** (solo, 15 months while employed) [all fetched & verified].
- Two-person proofs: **Everhood** (2), **Slay the Spire** (2 leads), **Cassette Beasts** (2 FT), **Sir, We Have an Orc Problem** (2 devs, 4+ months, $361,657 gross in 24h, 200K units in 20 days — Zukowski's Aug 2026 "golden age" case) [fetched & verified].
- Zukowski's current scope thesis (2025–26): "Build Fast" — successful "middle games" ship in under 9 months; "the scale of game development does NOT guarantee the scale of your success" (How Many Dudes: 7 devs, exactly 1 year, 242K units) [fetched & verified].
- Content-volume shape by wrapper: narrative RPG is writing-heavy/asset-light (Undertale: 32 solo months; Everhood: 2 people); deckbuilder is systems-heavy but content-cheap per hour ("a single new card deck can add 15+ hours" — Zukowski's core argument for why systems games out-earn "going around" games [fetched & verified]); survivors-like is the cheapest to build (VS: £1,100) but now the most craft-gated to launch.

## Synthesis: the three best wrapper candidates for game #2

**1. Story-rich roguelite action ("indie Hades with real bullet hell").** The only wrapper where the bullet-hell payload needs zero disguise and the market is *growing into* you: roguelite/roguelike are the top two tags among 50k+ sellers, and every existence proof from solo (Nova Drift, 396K units) to 4-person (Gungeon, 3M) to 20-person (Hades) cleared. The formula the data endorses: simple controls + deep build-crafting meta + a story reason to keep dying (Hades' narrative-on-death loop, God Mode's audience-widening). This is the highest-probability lane; its cost is that you're competing on polish in a busy field.

**2. Undertale-shape narrative RPG with bullet-hell combat.** RPG is the single most-demanded genre bucket in Zukowski's top-20 data, JRPG-classic structure is declining but *quirky story-rich indie RPG* is not, and the exact trick — story attracts, bullet hell delivers — is proven at 5–10M units (Undertale, near-solo) and re-proven at 2-person scale (Everhood, 8.2K Overwhelmingly Positive at $9.99, healthy enough to fund a sequel). It's the best expected-value-per-asset-dollar if the team's differentiator is writing/character; the risk is variance — this lane pays on distinctiveness, not on genre-shelf demand, and the middle between "Everhood outcome" and "obscurity" is thin.

**3. Deckbuilder × shooter hybrid (the contrarian bet).** Roguelike deckbuilder is Zukowski's highest-median genre with the lowest supply, Balatro just showed a solo dev converting the shape into 5M+ units, and *nobody has shipped the shmup hybrid* — you'd be attempting a Phase-1/2 "define the genre" move (his stated path for small teams: introduce the mechanic the second wave copies). Systems-heavy/asset-light suits 1–2 people. But it carries genuine design risk: no existence proof means the card-cadence/twitch-dodging marriage may simply not work, so prototype the combat loop before committing.

**Explicitly not recommended as the wrapper:** a pure survivors-like (Phase 4, >75% share held by three games, Zukowski warns first-timers off) — though its lesson (meta-progression as the casual bridge) should be stolen by whichever wrapper wins; and a straight shmup wrapper, which sits in the bottom median-revenue tier under its own flag.

## Contradictions / thin evidence

- **Zukowski's median-revenue rankings are chart-based**; the fetched text gives orderings, not dollar values per genre (except horror's $1,200 / $13,271). The dollar gap between "roguelike deckbuilder #1" and "shmup bottom-tier" is unquantified here.
- **VG Insights market reports were unreachable** — vginsights.com now 301s into Sensor Tower's JS-only app (WebFetch got an empty shell; archive.today fetch timed out into background without output). All VGI-derived numbers arrive second-hand through Zukowski's 2022 post.
- **GameDiscoverCo depth was limited to public posts** — the genre-shift post was fetched, but their paid per-genre revenue/hit-rate tables and any monster-taming or cozy analyses were not reachable (Substack search endpoints 404 via GET).
- **Everhood's 8,184 reviews is the English-filtered count** shown on the store page; the all-language total is somewhat higher. Everhood revenue is my Boxleiter-style inference, not a fetched figure. No sales figures exist in fetched sources for Everhood, JS&B, or Cassette Beasts.
- **Nova Drift has no Wikipedia article** (both URL forms 404); its numbers rest on Zukowski's interview post and Steam's review count, which corroborate each other.
- **"No deckbuilder×shmup / horror×shmup exists"** is an absence-of-evidence claim from fetched sources only — no search was possible to confirm the negative. FAITH's mechanical characterization is from my prior knowledge [unverified].
- **Tension inside Zukowski's own framework:** his Crafty Buildy thesis calls action/RPG "going around" games a bad bet, while his own top-20 data shows RPG as the most frequent hit genre — the reconciliation (hits are big-budget; *median* indie RPGs struggle) is my reading, not his stated claim.
